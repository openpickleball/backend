## Account deletion

ADR 0048 records these decisions (the maintainer's on #116). Account deletion is privacy-sensitive: it must remove all personal data ADR 0009's Retention section does not keep, and never change the Ledger.

- `DELETE /api/v1/accounts/me` (`delete_current_account`) needs a reauthentication within `ACCOUNT_REAUTHENTICATION_TIMEOUT` (allauth's `auth/reauthenticate` or `auth/2fa/reauthenticate`), read by `config.auth.require_recent_reauthentication` from the session behind the request (`auth_session`), so both clients work; a login alone does not count. Without one it is 403 `reauthentication_required` (`ReauthenticationRequired`). A staff or superuser Account gets 409. Success is 204, and the request's session is logged out.
- Delete an Account's personal data only through `apps.accounts.deletion.delete_account(account, actor=...)`, in its own transaction. It locks the Account row, then the Player row, then the report rows, then each Organizer before its membership. It deletes the Account's sessions (each `UserSession` row and the Django session it names), `EmailAddress` rows, MFA authenticators, `AgreementAcceptance` rows, OAuth tokens and grants (device grants included), its data export (`delete_exports_for`, ADR 0051), the Organizer Reports it filed (closing an open one first with rule `reporter_deleted`), the Organizer Invites and Club Invites addressed to its addresses or sent by it (`_delete_invites`, ADR 0054, ADR 0055), queued emails to its addresses, and the admin log entries tied to it; removes its memberships and Club Roles through `apps.audit.access` (so the other admins of each Organizer and Club get the member removed or Club admin removed security notice, #232, ADR 0057; the deleted Account gets none); and records one `account.delete` Audit Event (`after`: `account_kept`, `player_kept`). Every event it records has no actor when the actor is the deleted Account.
- A Player nothing protects is deleted. Any other Player stays with its OPEN Code: display name `DELETED_PLAYER_NAME`, legal name, gender division, and city and region empty, birth year and month NULL, every privacy setting private. The Account is hard-deleted only when nothing protects it and it owns no Partner Application; otherwise it stays deactivated with a placeholder email at `deleted.invalid` (`deleted_email()`), an unusable password, no staff flags, groups, or permissions, and no `last_login`. The placeholder frees the address, so the same email signs up again: that is how a player fixes a birth year and month or a gender division entered wrong (ADR 0023).
- The Ledger never changes: Matches (with their stored match type and rating format), participants, confirmations, vouches, flags, batches, ratings, rating history, and the engine snapshot stay, so a replay gives the same ratings. A new link to an Account or Player that uses `PROTECT` keeps more rows; a new model that holds an Account's or Player's personal data needs a step in `delete_account` and a test.
- No new Match names a Player whose Account is inactive: the first-party create (`_resolve_teams`), the CSV upload, `record_match()` (before validation, and again under the Players' `FOR KEY SHARE` locks in its write transaction), and `edit_match()` (under the same locks) refuse it as `unknown_player` (`refuse_inactive_players`). A correction may keep the players of the Match it corrects. It still derives its own rating format (ADR 0024), so a deleted player's empty division makes it overall doubles.
- Lock order: `delete_account` takes the Account (`FOR UPDATE`), the Player (`FOR UPDATE`), every Organizer of its memberships and reports by id (`FOR UPDATE`), then the membership and report rows. `record_match` and `edit_match` take the acting Account `FOR KEY SHARE` first (`hold_submitting_account`), `report_organizer` takes the reporter's Account the same way before the Organizer (`apps.accounts.locks.hold_active_account`), each checking it is still active, `create_player` checks `is_active` under its Account lock, `create_organizer` locks the acting Account `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` and checks it is active, and `accept_invite` and the Organizer admin operations in `apps.clubs.self_service` (rename, invite, member list, role change, removal, verification request) hold the acting Account with `hold_active_account` before they lock the Organizer (ADR 0054), `create_club` locks the acting Account `FOR NO KEY UPDATE`, and the Club admin operations and `accept_club_invite` in `apps.clubs.club_self_service` hold the acting Account (and, on accept, the invite's sender) before they lock the Club (ADR 0055), so none lands a new row on a deleted Account (an Organizer's correction still keeps its corrected Match's submitter link, which may be a kept, deleted Account). Keep these orders.
- The Account admin's delete page and `delete_selected` run `delete_account(account, actor=request.user)` for each Account (#317): `UserAdmin.delete_queryset` calls `delete_model` per Account, each in its own transaction with its `admin.delete` event (`deletes_each_through_delete_model`, read by `AdminAuditMixin`), and `delete_model` locks the Account and checks `has_delete_permission` again first. Nobody deletes a staff or superuser Account there (as the API's 409), nor one a deletion already cleared; only a superuser deletes one with a group or permission. `get_deleted_objects` lists only the selected Accounts, so Django's cascade and its protected list never apply, `log_deletions` writes no admin log entry (its text is the email), and the success message names the row id, not the email. When the Account was deleted meanwhile, `delete_model` rolls back the `admin.delete` event. Never let an admin path delete an Account any other way.
- An Account that must have two-factor authentication (an Organizer member, ADR 0043) and has no method gets 403 `mfa_required` on the delete, as on every operation: it sets up a method first. Never exempt the delete from the MFA check.
- A session with no `UserSession` row (from before #100) is not found; the inactive Account and unusable password make Django refuse it, and the session sweep (`sweep_sessions`, the retention task) deletes it when it expires. allauth's rate limit cache keys age out within an hour; backups age out within 30 days (#67).
- Known limits (ADR 0048): a signup hold in an anonymous session that never logged in stays until that session expires; a login that passed its password check just before the deletion commits can still write `last_login` and a session on the kept row, which the inactive Account then makes useless.
